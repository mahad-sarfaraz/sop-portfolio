Aerodynamic Performance Optimization

Fixed-wing UAV optimized for environmental monitoring at 150 km/h cruise and 5km altitude. The design objective was a 10% L/D improvement. Both conceptual designs exceeded it by a factor of 6.

Key Results

  • Conceptual UAV I: 59.1% improvement in L/D ratio at alpha = 2 degrees cruise condition, using S2091-101-83 airfoil with forward-swept wing geometry.
  • Conceptual UAV II: 68.5% improvement in L/D ratio at alpha = 2 degrees, using E387 airfoil, the most widely wind-tunnel tested low Reynolds number airfoil, validated at NASA Langley, Delft, Stuttgart, and Princeton.
  • Induced drag reduced by approximately 40% through aspect ratio increase from 13.0 to 26.0, taper ratio of 0.41 approximating elliptical spanwise lift distribution, and forward sweep angle of 6.48 degrees.
  • XFOIL results validated against Anderson experimental data for NACA 2412, confirming model reliability in attached flow conditions.

Methodology

  • 2D airfoil analysis performed in XFOIL at Re = 3 x 10^6, sweeping angle of attack from -5 to 15 degrees across candidate airfoils.
  • 3D full aircraft analysis conducted in XFLR5 using four analytical methods: 3D Panel Method, Lifting Line Theory (LLT), inviscid VLM1, and viscous VLM2. Viscous VLM2 was selected as the primary method for incorporating boundary layer corrections from 2D polars.
  • Wing geometry modifications included wingspan increase from 20m to 25m, forward sweep, increased washout twist of -2 degrees at tip, and optimized taper ratio of 0.41.
  • Stability tradeoff identified: L/D optimization produced stronger nose-down pitching moments, flagged for tail redesign in future design stages.

Tools

  • XFLR5 for 3D aerodynamic simulation using Panel Method, LLT, VLM1, and VLM2
  • XFOIL for 2D airfoil analysis with viscous boundary layer solver
  • WebPlotDigitizer for experimental data extraction and XFOIL validation
