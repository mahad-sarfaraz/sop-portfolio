Blended Wing Body with Adaptive Camber Elevons

Lambda-wing BWB aircraft with morphing elevons, achieving 15.6% L/D improvement and 18.7% vortex drag reduction. Research published at national and international conferences and accepted in press at Springer Nature.

Awards and Publications

  • 1st Place Best Paper Award, NSCADF'25 (National Conference) for "Morphing Elevons in Blended Wing Body Aircraft".
  • In Press, Springer Nature (Expected August 2026): "Morphing Aerodynamics on a Lambda-Wing Blended-Wing-Body: Adaptive Camber Elevons for Green Mobility", ICSPER'25 (International Conference).
  • Published in peer-reviewed journal for "Designing a Flying Wing with Adaptive Camber Elevon".

Key Results

  • Processed and visualized VSPAERO aerodynamic performance data in MATLAB , generating CL, CD and L/D vs angle of attack plots across an angle of attack sweep from -10 to +10 degrees.
  • 15.6% improvement in L/D ratio compared to conventional fixed wing aircraft, confirmed through OpenVSP and XFLR5 simulations.
  • 18.7% reduction in vortex drag at alpha = 8 degrees relative to analytical baseline, achieved through 40 degree lambda wing integration.
  • 13.5% potential fuel reduction based on aerodynamic efficiency gains.
  • Calculated L/D ratio of 18.64 at alpha = 8 degrees using NACA 23012 airfoil with finite-wing correction and sweep correction applied.
  • CL of 0.738 at alpha = 8 degrees with induced drag coefficient of 0.0246 and total drag coefficient of 0.0396.
  • Kerf-rib elevon mechanism achieving plus or minus 12 degrees camber modulation with a structural factor of safety of 2.5.

Design and Methodology

  • Conceptual design inspired by the Boeing-NASA X-48B, initiated in 2D AutoCAD before progressing to 3D parametric modeling in OpenVSP.
  • NACA 23012 and 23021 airfoils selected for the main wing to enable smooth camber adjustment during elevon morphing. NACA 0012 used for elevon surfaces.
  • 40 degree lambda wing planform integrated to manage leading-edge vortices and reduce spanwise drag penalty.
  • Aerodynamic analysis conducted in XFLR5 and OpenVSP across angles of attack from 0 to 15 degrees, evaluating lift, drag, efficiency, delta-Cp, vorticity, and wake-line parameters.
  • Adaptive camber elevons use kerf-rib morphing structure allowing localized camber variation without hydraulic actuation, reducing weight and complexity.

Tools

  • OpenVSP for 3D parametric geometry modeling and aerodynamic analysis
  • XFLR5 for lift, drag, and efficiency simulation across angle of attack sweep
  • AutoCAD for initial 2D conceptual design
